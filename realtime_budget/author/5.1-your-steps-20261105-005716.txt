[textarea Your steps]
Start with the reset log. There are TMON overruns in each task, along with IWDG resets. All the resets happen during intense CAN reception, and the IWDG resets are in units where LSI runs fast.
The WCET values of each task should be their largest net execution times in three reference traces – SpeedCtrl (150 µs), CanComm (380 µs), Monitor (780 µs), Supervisor (240 µs), and NvM (3900 µs). The interrupt WCET values will be as follows: ADC (9.0 µs), CAN1_RX0 (52.0 µs), and SysTick (3.0 µs).
The TMON values include more than the execution time of the task itself, when CAN messages are received. The `CAN1_RX0_IRQHandler` in `can_if_glue.c` does not have any TMON brackets; hence, the execution time is charged to whatever task is executing. TAG-01 Appendix A is obsolete and requires an update.
Determine the interrupt rate of CAN worst case scenario based on the actual traffic in the system. Bank 1 filter, programmed with an ID `0x7DF` with mask `0x700`, will accept all ID `0x700` up to `0x7FF`. VN-07 can accommodate those frames in a back-to-back fashion. Minimum time for 8 bytes long frame will be 111 bits at 500 kbit/s, which gives us 222 µs. Use 222 µs for analysis since 1 ms in workshop trace is not worst case.
Based on TAG-01 Section 3, the largest times for monitoring are SpeedCtrl 257 µs, CanComm 539 µs, Monitor 1150 µs, Supervisor 347 µs, and NvM 5533 µs. Assign each budget between their largest time and 25% over it. Then use Section 4 to determine whether each task satisfies its deadline with the new budgets.
Finally, determine the largest time interval between watchdog refreshments to be 20 ms added by the Supervisor response time. Select the IWDG prescaler and reload values in such a way that the timeout is larger than the time interval at the maximum possible frequency of LSI from the datasheet 47 kHz, and at the same time is less than or equal to 80 ms at 17 kHz.
