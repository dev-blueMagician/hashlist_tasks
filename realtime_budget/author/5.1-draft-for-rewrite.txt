Start with the reset log. Every task has TMON overruns, there are four IWDG resets, and every reset came with heavy CAN reception (246 to 371 frames in the 100 ms before it).
Take each task's WCET as its largest net execution time in the three reference traces: SpeedCtrl 150, CanComm 380, Monitor 780, Supervisor 240 and NvM 3900 µs. The interrupt WCETs are ADC 9.0, CAN1_RX0 52.0 and SysTick 3.0 µs.
TMON measures more than the task's own time when CAN frames arrive. The new CAN1_RX0_IRQHandler in can_if_glue.c has no TMON brackets, so its time is charged to whatever job is running, and TAG-01 appendix A is out of date.
Find the fastest rate at which the CAN receive interrupt can fire from the bus rules, not from the traces (TAG-01 section 2.3). The bank 1 filter (0x7DF, mask 0x700) accepts every ID from 0x700 to 0x7FF, and VN-07 section 4.5 lets those frames fill the bus back to back with STmin 0 while any ECU is being reprogrammed. Without stuff bits the shortest 8-byte frame is 111 bit times (VN-07 section 5.1), 222 µs at 500 kbit/s. The 1 ms STmin and the roughly 600 µs shortest gap in the workshop trace are both slower than the bus allows.
Find the fastest ADC interrupt rate from the timer configuration, not from the traces. The PWM mode tables in src/mcal/Pwm_Cfg.c are raw register writes: the LowRipple mode writes 0 to 0x40010030, which is TIM1_RCR, where the Run mode writes 1. TIM1 counts center-aligned (CR1 0xA1) with its update event as TRGO (CR2 0x20), and ADC1 starts its injected conversions on TIM1_TRGO (ADC1_CR2 0x00110001). RM0090 section 17.3.3 says the repetition counter counts both the overflow and the underflow in center-aligned mode, so with RCR = 0 the ADC runs twice per 20 kHz period, every 25 µs. speed_ctrl.c selects LowRipple below 900 rpm (Cal.PwmModeLowEnter_rpm), which is inside the SRS speed range of 600 to 5200 rpm. No trace runs that slowly, so all three show 50 µs.
Compute each task's largest monitored time with TAG-01 section 3: SpeedCtrl 257, CanComm 646, Monitor 1254, Supervisor 399 and NvM 6218 µs. Set each budget between that value and 25 % above it, then check every deadline with TAG-01 section 4. With the 3.2.0 priorities, NvM misses its deadline from about 8 % above the monitored times, so the budgets have to stay close to them (for example 2 % above), or the Supervisor's priority has to be raised.
Finally, work out the longest gap between two watchdog refreshes: the 20 ms Supervisor period plus the Supervisor's response time with the new budgets (TAG-01 section 5), about 28.0 ms with 2 % budgets. Choose the IWDG prescaler and reload so that the timeout is longer than this gap at the datasheet's LSI maximum of 47 kHz, and no more than 80 ms at its minimum of 17 kHz. That leaves about 1316 to 1360 LSI periods; prescaler 0 with reload 335 gives 1344 periods, 28.6 ms at 47 kHz and 79.1 ms at 17 kHz.
