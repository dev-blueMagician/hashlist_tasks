HDL feature development (Verilog)

Tasks where the model changes a working Verilog block the way an RTL engineer would: add a feature, port it to a new memory or bus, add a mode. A withheld testbench you write grades it.

Agent box: Debian with Icarus Verilog 13, Verilator 5.048, make and python3, internet off. The design files and your smoke testbench are under /app/data. make -s -C /app/data runs the smoke test.

What the model hands in: the design files under /app/data, as it leaves them. Keep the top module's name and ports fixed. The modules under it are the model's to change.

Grader (already written, don't edit it):
The RTL must be synthesizable and name nothing outside itself: no initial , no delays, no system tasks, no hierarchical names, no import / export (DPI), no wait , fork or unbounded loops, and every event control is @* or posedge/negedge edges.

Then every withheld testbench in verifier/rtl.json runs once on Icarus and 16 times on Verilator, with every flop and memory powering up random. Each run gets a fresh stimulus seed ( +seed ).

A check passes only if it passes on every run. So a design that sniffs X, calibrates itself to the bench, or depends on its power-up state fails.

What you hand in:
Base RTL that works today, 150 to 1500 lines. It can be your own, or an open core under Apache-2.0, Solderpad, BSD or MIT (put the licence in NOTICE).

The change request, with the full interface and timing contract. The model must not have to guess anything your bench checks.

A smoke testbench that passes on the base and says nothing about the new behaviour.

A hidden testbench with a reference model, random traffic driven from +seed , and at least two parameter sets. It prints CHECK <nonce> <name> PASS|FAIL and DONE <nonce> . Verilog-2005 or SystemVerilog that both Icarus and Verilator run.

verifier/rtl.json. List under must the checks the base design already passes, the behaviour the change must not break. Failing one scores 0, and they pay nothing.

References: gold (scores 1.00), naive_base (the design as handed over, scores 0.00), and at least two wrong changes a competent engineer would make first: a plausible partial feature, and the most likely shortcut. Each must score lower than gold.

What makes a good task: a fact the model has to work out rather than read off. A latency that only a characterization report reveals, a corner the datasheet buries, an interaction between the new feature and an existing mode. Strong models solve plain feature-adds from the prompt alone, so we reject those, along with pure bug fixes and anything copied from a public repo's PR without a changed spec.

Before you submit: the grader check on your task must say SOUND, and the frontier models should pass it sometimes but not always.

What good looks like

Ten runs of one task. A good task spreads the scores.

#1 0.20 answered without opening the capture

#2 0.60 right cause, number off by a factor

#3 0.00 never wrote an answer

#4 1.00 read the capture, derived the number

#5 0.60 right cause, number off by a factor

#6 0.20 took the first value that fit

#7 0.80 correct, missed one quantity

#8 0.00 ran out of steps

#9 0.40 right format, every number wrong

#10 0.60 right cause, number off by a factor

Strong models should score higher than weak ones, and the correct answer higher than any common mistake. A skilled person should be able to do it with what the model is given.
